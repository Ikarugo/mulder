## Analysis of Misses and Errors

### One system, one analyst

The catalog phase's JSON registered the four images as a single system, `cfreds_2015_data_leakage`, as it did in run 2 and did not in run 1 (four systems, four extraction sessions, a cross-system phase). Everything downstream follows from that: one 60-turn analyst holding the PC's 104,709-line `tsk.filelist`, the 5,209-line `registry.system` and three sticks' worth of sources in one 262K-token window, and no cross-system phase at all ("only 1 system(s) in catalog"). The first analyst paged `tsk.filelist` ten and then five rows at a time for ten minutes (`after_id` 0 to 120, 01:12:00–01:21:49) and hit the Bedrock context ceiling at 51 tool calls; its auto-compacted successor did the same and hit it again at 42; the third, given a compacted summary, ran 31 calls and wrote "no additional findings were identified beyond those already submitted". Four findings were accepted in 236 extraction turns (run 1: 14 in 12 continuations), and the alternative-narrative agent's one `update_finding` and one turn-limit continuation added none. The 17 failed first-batch tasks (bad `bulk_extractor` scanner names, `detect_masquerading` at offset 0, Chainsaw, Amcache, Prefetch) were mostly recovered by the follow-up plan; the shortage was analysis, not extraction.

### Read and not reported (items 2, 8, 20; 5)

`CurrentVersion` (with `ProductName`), `TimeZoneInformation` (twice), `ez.shimcache` (twice, 307 lines) and `registry.ntuser.informant` were all fetched and none produced a sentence: no OS, no timezone, no CCleaner or Eraser, no name. The disc was extracted from five times and analysed four times and is absent from the report except as a filename. Run 1 turned the same registry reads into "Windows 7 Professional", Eraser at 15:12:28Z and the resignation-letter filename; this run turned them into nothing, and items 2, 5 and 8 go from PARTIAL to MISSED.

### The USB device family (items 3, 4, 12; FP 1)

The best material in the report and the first time a Qwen run has read `USBSTOR`: `Disk&Ven_SanDisk&Prod_Cruzer_Fit&Rev_2.01`, both serials, the FriendlyName, the 13:37:59Z LastWrite, the 'Authorized USB' label from rm1's listing. It is then assembled wrong: one device with two "instances" instead of two devices, no filesystem for either, no serial tied to a label or an image, no per-device connection time, and the 'Authorized USB' volume mounted on `D:` with the PC's `S data` folder on it. Items 3, 4 and 12 rise from MISSED to PARTIAL on the raw facts and stop there on the assembly; FP 1 is the assembly carried into the narrative.

### Masquerading without a medium (items 7, 15, 16; FP 2)

`detect_masquerading` returned 17 lines on rm2 and the finding quotes it, which run 1 never had. The finding does not say where the files are: "Files discovered on the system", "in the file system". No RM2, no FAT32, no 'IAMAN $_@', no count, no mapping to the five originals, and the disc, which holds the same names and was extracted from in-run, is never connected. The finding's time range, taken from the listing's earliest and latest timestamps, becomes the narrative's "December 16, 2014, to March 24, 2015" preparation window and the strategic finding that the data was prepared "over a period of several months", three months before the PC existed.

### Nothing from the cloud, the tools, the searches, the OST or the share (items 8–11, 13, 14, 18, 19)

Run 1's two FOUND items (Google Drive and iCloud, every timestamp exact) and its Eraser timestamp came from `get_timeline` and `search` over the MFT and NTUSER; this run's 24 `get_timeline` calls are almost all the Mar 24 13:00–14:00 hour around the USB write, and its 58 searches are "USB", "USBSTOR", the two serials, "Secret Project Data", "Cruzer", "informant" across ten sources, "diary" and "zip". Nothing cloud, nothing anti-forensic, no search term, no February, no OST, no share. Eight items blank where run 1 had five with content.

### Findings management

Six `submit_finding` calls, the first two rejected for missing fields and resubmitted as the INFO USB finding; four accepted, none merged, one `deduplicate_findings` dry run, one `update_finding`. Each finding cites one source (`tsk.filelist`, `registry.usrclass.informant`, `tsk.masquerade`, `registry.system`), which is why the narrative gate saw five distinct names cited out of 36 and why the report has no cross-source correlation at all: the USB finding, the browse finding and the masquerade finding are never joined into one account of which device received what.
